# Annotationes — the Spiritual Exercises in the twentieth century

A research apparatus on the modern reception of the *Exercitia spiritualia* of Ignatius of Loyola,
in the orbit of [Ignatiana](https://ignatian-research.netlify.app/). The text of the Exercises stands at
the centre, paragraph by paragraph on the grid [1]–[370]; the twentieth century's commentaries,
translations, papal documents, journal articles and literary readings stand in the margin, each where
it touches the book, filterable by language, decade, genre and access.

Live at <https://annotationes.netlify.app/> (deployed from `main`). The design document is [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

## What is in

- The trilingual text (Spanish autograph, Vulgate 1548, Mullan 1914), taken over from Ignatiana.
- A German layer: Alfred Feder's translation (Regensburg 1922), public domain in the US and in Germany,
  paragraph-exact on [1]–[370] (`data/feder.json`). Pipeline: `tools/feder_extract.py` (ABBYY OCR of the
  microfilm → paragraphs without running heads and footnotes), `tools/feder_fix.py` (dictionary-checked
  corrections, log in `data/src/feder_corrections.json`), `tools/feder_align.py` with the editorial anchor
  table `tools/feder_anchors.json`, and `tools/feder_check.py` (length check against the Spanish).
  Feder's 108 endnotes (pp. 164–185) as a German commentary layer, placed by the page of each call
  (`tools/feder_notes.py` → `data/feder_notes.json`).
- Longridge 1919 as a commentary layer (taken over from Ignatiana; text loaded on demand).
- Rickaby's notes (*Spanish and English, with a Continuous Commentary*, 2nd ed. 1923; first ed. 1915) as a
  second English commentary layer (`data/rickaby_exx.json`), built by `tools/rickaby_extract.py` from the
  Google Books PDF (F7kHAQAAIAAJ, not in the repository): columns and notes separated, each set of notes
  placed on the paragraphs whose Spanish it follows. Rickaby rearranges the book; the work page says how.
  His translation is a second English text in the reader ("en · Rickaby", `data/rickaby_text.json`), cut onto
  the grid by `tools/rickaby_text.py`: between the openings found in his Spanish column, with the cuts steered by
  word overlap with Mullan 1914 (only as a guide; no word of Mullan is used).
- Joyce, *A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man* (1916), the retreat chapter: four passages.
- Pius XI, *Summorum Pontificum* (1922) and *Mens nostra* (1929): key passages from the Acta
  Apostolicae Sedis.
- Przywara, *Deus semper maior* (1938–40): a page anchor table for all three volumes, 148 anchors,
  read in the lending scans (`data/src/przywara_anchors.json`); and his own index of the numbers of the
  Exercises (vol. III, pp. 438–440), transcribed by hand (`data/src/przywara_register.txt`, parsed by
  `tools/przywara_register.py`): the 130 entries that reach beyond the anchor table are glosses of their own.
- Jung, *Collected Works*: 28 passages on Ignatius and the Exercises, read and placed by paragraph,
  summaries only (`data/src/jung_cw.json`). Jung quotes the Exercises in Rickaby's second edition.
- 75 articles of the *Zeitschrift für Aszese und Mystik* (1926–1944) and *Geist und Leben*
  (1947–1978) from the publisher's free chronological archive, linked and assigned from their titles;
  selected articles of the *Revue d'ascétique et de mystique* (1920–23) and of *Studies in the
  Spirituality of Jesuits* (1969–78).
- Spanish and Italian before 1931 (`tools/es_it.py` → `data/src/es_it.json`): eleven articles of *Razón y
  Fe* (1904–1919, found by searching vols. 1–61 in the Internet Archive), Arturo Codina, *Los orígenes de
  los Ejercicios* (1926), placed by its own subject index, and Carlo Torti's Italian commentary (1909),
  placed by its section heads; Benjamín Marcos, *San Ignacio de Loyola* (1923, Project Gutenberg), read in full.
- Seven reading paths (`data/paths.json`, `#/paths`) and an introduction essay (`#/intro`).
- Bibliographic records of the protected core of the century (Rahner, Fessard, Balthasar, Barthes,
  Certeau, Jung and others), marked as such.
- Public-domain plates.

## Access tiers

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Public domain in the US (published before 1931): the text, or the passages a gloss quotes, is shown. |
| B | Free to read on the publisher's site: linked, nothing copied. |
| C | Protected: only an anchor table (paragraph → page) will ship; full text on the reader's own copy. |
| D | Reference only. |

## Data and tools

- `data/works.json` — one record per work (hand-maintained).
- `data/src/glosses_curated.json` — hand-written glosses.
- `data/src/journals.json` — generated by `tools/journals.py` (`crawl`, then `assign`).
- `data/glosses.json` — the index the site reads, built by `python tools/build_glosses.py`, which
  checks ranges, work ids and that quotations come only from public-domain works.
- `data/plates.json`, `assets/plates/` — built by `python tools/build_plates.py`.

## Running it

Any static server, e.g. `python -m http.server 9051` in this folder, then open
`http://localhost:9051/`.
